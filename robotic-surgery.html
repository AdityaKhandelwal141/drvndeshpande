The Process

How Robotic-Assisted Surgery Works

It's important to understand that the robot does not perform the surgery. Your surgeon remains in complete control, using the robotic system as an advanced tool for precision.

1

3D CT Scan Mapping

Before surgery, a detailed 3D CT scan of your joint is taken. This creates a digital twin of your unique anatomy.

2

Pre-Surgical Planning

Your surgeon uses the 3D model to plan the exact size, position, and alignment of your implant down to the millimeter.

3

Live Guided Execution

During surgery, the robotic arm provides real-time feedback, preventing the surgeon from moving outside the planned boundaries and ensuring perfect precision.

Why It Matters

Benefits of Robotic Precision

Robotic-assisted technology helps us achieve a level of accuracy that is difficult to achieve with traditional manual instruments.

  • ✓
    Highly Accurate Implant Alignment: Better alignment means the implant feels more natural.
  • ✓
    Less Soft Tissue Damage: The robotic arm stays exactly within the planned bone boundaries, sparing surrounding muscles and ligaments.
  • ✓
    Faster Recovery: Less trauma to the body often leads to reduced pain and a quicker return to daily life.
  • ✓
    Longer Implant Lifespan: Perfect alignment reduces uneven wear and tear, potentially increasing the lifespan of the joint replacement.

Clearing the Air

Robotic Surgery: Myths vs Facts

Myth

"The robot performs the surgery by itself."

Fact

The robot is a tool controlled by the surgeon. The surgeon makes all the decisions and guides the robotic arm.

Myth

"Robotic surgery is an experimental procedure."

Fact

Robotic joint replacement is a proven, FDA-approved technology that has been used successfully worldwide for many years.

Myth

"It means my surgery will take much longer."

Fact

While setup takes a little time, the actual bone preparation and implant placement are highly efficient and often result in less time under anesthesia.
